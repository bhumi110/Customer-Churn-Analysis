# Customer Churn Analysis Dashboard

## 📊 Project Overview

This project analyzes customer churn for a telecommunications company using Power BI.

The objective is to understand the overall churn rate and identify customer characteristics and service patterns associated with higher churn.

The analysis focuses on customer tenure, contract type, internet service, payment method, and dependent status.


## 🎯 Business Problem

The telecom company is experiencing customer churn and wants to understand:

- How significant is the churn problem?
- Which customer groups have higher churn rates?
- Does customer tenure relate to churn?
- How does contract type relate to churn?
- Are churn rates different across internet services and payment methods?

The analysis is intended to help stakeholders identify customer segments that may require further investigation for retention strategies.

## ❓ Business Questions

1. What is the overall customer churn rate?
2. How many customers have churned?
3. Does contract type relate to customer churn?
4. Does customer tenure relate to churn?
5. How does churn vary by internet service?
6. How does churn vary by payment method?
7. Does dependent status show differences in churn rates?

## 📈 Dashboard Analysis

The dashboard includes:

- Overall Customer Churn
- Churn Rate by Contract Type
- Churn Rate by Tenure
- Churn Rate by Internet Service
- Churn Rate by Payment Method
- Churn Rate by Dependent Status
- Gender-based filtering


## 🔍 Key Insights

- Overall customer churn is approximately **27%**, with 1,869 customers having churned.
- **Month-to-month customers have the highest churn rate at 43%**, compared with 11% for one-year and 3% for two-year contracts.
- Customers with **0–12 months of tenure have the highest churn rate at 47%**, with churn declining across longer-tenure groups.
- **Fiber-optic users have a 42% churn rate**, compared with 19% for DSL users and 7% for customers without internet service.
- Customers using **electronic checks have the highest churn rate among payment methods at 45%**.
- Customers without dependents have a higher observed churn rate (**33%**) than customers with dependents (**7%**).

> Note: These findings describe patterns and associations in the dataset and do not establish that any individual factor directly causes customer churn.
